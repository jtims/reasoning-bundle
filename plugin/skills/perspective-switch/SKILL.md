# Perspective Switch

The flaw is usually obvious from every perspective but your own. This skill evaluates a subject
from several independent viewpoints at once, then synthesizes where they agree and where they pull
against each other.

Derived from the research-swarm perspective-diverse verification pattern and the Frontier Operating
Manual S6 inversion move.

## Evaluate each perspective independently, then compare (single-context approximation)

Default set - adapt to the subject:
- **Technical** - correctness, maintainability, failure modes.
- **User** - the person on the other end; what breaks their flow.
- **Business** - cost, revenue, strategic fit, opportunity cost.
- **Risk / security** - what an adversary, or a bad day, does to this.
- **Operations** - who runs it at 3am, and with what signal.

Add domain viewpoints as needed (legal, compliance, support). Work each viewpoint on its own terms:
state its single strongest objection and its one must-have BEFORE reading or writing any other
viewpoint, so later perspectives are not anchored on earlier ones. This is a single-context
approximation of parallel evaluation, not true isolation - in one response the model still writes
them in sequence. For a genuinely blind, parallel pass on a high-stakes call, fan out one agent per
perspective (each seeing only its own lens) and synthesize the returns.

## Synthesize

- **Consensus** - what every perspective agrees on (usually the safe part).
- **Tensions** - where perspectives conflict; these are the real decisions - name them explicitly,
  do not average them away.
- **Meta-insight** - what only becomes visible once the viewpoints sit side by side.

## Boundary

Advisory. When the tensions resolve to a choice among options, hand off to `decision-matrix`; when
a single conclusion needs attacking rather than viewing, use `devils-advocate`.
